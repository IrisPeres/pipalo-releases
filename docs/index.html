What is Pipalo?

A small pal that lives in the corner of your screen

Pipalo is a background app for your Mac. Pip, a pixel blob, shows how much of your AI budget you have used and nudges you to look after yourself while you work.

Pipalo is a background app that polls on a timer, without a model. Every so often it reads a few local files and your own tools, does simple arithmetic, and decides whether Pip should speak. No AI model runs inside it, there is no server and there is no account.

It reads token counts, model names, file sizes and timestamps. It never reads what you type or what Claude writes back.

The main feature

Token consumption, under water

Dashboards are easy to ignore. A mascot slowly drowning in your usage is not. The water level is your monthly budget, read from the files Claude Code already keeps on your machine.

What Pip does

Productivity

  • Token Wave dashboard: Pip fills up as you use your monthly Claude Code budget, and panics when you burn tokens fast
  • Context gauge: a small bar beside Pip shows how full your active session's context window is, with a list of every active session and whether its cache is warm
  • Token tips: only while a session has grown big. Names the one command that fits (/compact, /recap, or a new session with a copyable handoff prompt) and spots costly habits such as model switches, cache misses, Opus-heavy use, a long CLAUDE.md and subagent fan-out
  • Pull request reviews: periodic reminders for PRs waiting for you, and teams you choose
  • Jira: sprint end dates and tickets assigned to you that are blocked
  • Calendars: connect several, for example Work and Personal. Alerts before meetings and when they move or are cancelled, and a meeting that is in both calendars alerts once
  • Slack, Teams, Mail and Outlook: a nudge when unread messages or emails have waited a while. It reads only the number on the app's Dock icon, never a message
  • Daily digest: one counts-only bubble at times you choose, such as "Slack 4 · Mail 12 · next meeting 10:30". A Details button opens the full picture, and Copy prompt gives you a line to paste into Claude to summarise your email. Pipalo never reads the email

Wellbeing

  • Hourly movement: stand up and walk every hour
  • Micro-breaks: posture checks every 30 minutes
  • 20-20-20 eye rest: look 20 feet away for 20 seconds, with a live countdown
  • Hydration: a drink of water every 45 minutes, and Pip drinks along
  • Fruit and lunch: an apple at 10:30, a banana at 16:00, lunch at 13:00
  • Sun walk: a lunchtime walk suggestion, only when the forecast is sunny
  • Screen time: after 90 minutes without a real break, a nudge to step away
  • End of day: at 18:00 Pip yawns and tells you to wrap up
  • Pomodoro: optional focus sessions that hold every other nudge
  • Your own settings: every reminder can be switched off or retimed, they stay quiet during meetings, and skipping never nags
  • Pip's size: small, medium or large, from the menu

Everything stays on your laptop

  • No account to create and no sign-in.
  • No telemetry, analytics, crash reporting or auto-update.
  • Usage comes from the local files Claude Code writes, never from a server of ours.
  • Jira and calendar credentials are encrypted in the macOS Keychain.
  • No keystroke logging, no screen capture, no webcam.
  • A Permissions panel lists everything Pipalo reads and where anything is sent, with a switch for each.

This page follows the same rule: no scripts from other sites, no trackers.
